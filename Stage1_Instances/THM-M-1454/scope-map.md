# Scope map

## Preserved theorem family

The intake preserves GMRES for finite-dimensional nonsymmetric linear systems as the named family.
A later statement phase may select a canonical root only after an immutable primary-source passage
and all incorporated definitions are mapped and independently reviewed. Candidate components, none
yet credited as the theorem, are:

- a finite-dimensional real or complex inner-product space, or the paper's real `N x N` matrices;
- a nonsingular linear operator or matrix `A`, right-hand side `f`, and initial approximation `x0`;
- the initial residual `r0 = f - A x0` and Krylov space generated by `r0, A r0, ...`;
- an Arnoldi orthonormal basis and its Hessenberg relation;
- the iterate in `x0 + K_k` minimizing the residual norm;
- exact termination and lucky-breakdown claims for unrestarted GMRES; and
- separate convergence results and counterexamples for restarted GMRES(m).

## Decisions required at statement freeze

The statement phase must freeze all of the following from an approved source rather than from a
generic numerical-linear-algebra convention:

1. The exact source edition, result locator, incorporated definitions, proof boundary, correction
   history, and independent review.
2. Whether the root is the minimal-residual property, Algorithm 3 correctness, Proposition 1 or 2,
   Corollary 3, the restarted method, Theorem 5, Corollary 6, or a checked conjunction.
3. The scalar field, dimension index, norms and inner products, and whether `A` is square,
   nonsingular, diagonalizable, positive real, preconditioned, or otherwise constrained.
4. The exact definitions of the Krylov space, Arnoldi basis and breakdown, Hessenberg matrices,
   least-squares minimizer, iterate, residual, minimal polynomial, and convergence.
5. Whether existence or uniqueness of the least-squares minimizer is asserted or built into the
   algorithm data, and how choices are represented in Lean.
6. Whether arithmetic is exact or floating-point; the 1986 theoretical claims are exact-arithmetic
   mathematics and do not automatically prove numerical stability of an implementation.
7. The ordered binders, dependent dimensions, hypotheses, conclusion, and logic strength.

## Degenerate and boundary cases

Source review must explicitly dispose of dimension zero; `r0 = 0`; singular `A`; `k = 0`; `k > N`;
an Arnoldi zero vector; a Krylov space that stops growing; nonunique least-squares minimizers; zero
or repeated eigenvalues; non-diagonalizable matrices; `m = 0`, `m = 1`, and `m >= N` for restarted
GMRES; and the distinction between exact-arithmetic termination, residual monotonicity, convergence,
stagnation, and finite-precision behavior.

## Substitution exclusions

- Arnoldi orthogonalization or a generic Krylov-space definition alone is not GMRES correctness.
- A theorem saying an explicitly chosen point minimizes a supplied function cannot replace the
  existence, construction, and source-defined iterate claims.
- Corollary 3 for unrestarted GMRES cannot be broadened to restarted GMRES(m); the source gives a
  stationary GMRES(1) example.
- Convergence under positive-real or spectral hypotheses cannot be stated without those hypotheses.
- A symmetric positive-definite conjugate-gradient result, MINRES, GCR, ORTHODIR, or a scalar toy
  system is not the requested nonsymmetric GMRES family.
- A numerical experiment, executable solver, tolerance result, or floating-point test is not an
  exact theorem proof.
- Generic matrix multiplication, Gram-Schmidt, projection, span, or norm APIs provide no root
  theorem credit.
- The repository label `verified` supplies no human-source or machine-proof evidence.

No canonical Lean target, expression fingerprint, checked alternate encoding, discovery protocol,
obligation registry, or proof state is frozen at intake.
