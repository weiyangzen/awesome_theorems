# Scope map

## Metadata boundary

The authoritative repository material gives only the title "simple theories" and the sentence
"classification of simple theories". A simple theory is a class of complete first-order theories,
and "classification" can refer to several definitions, characterizations, structural theorems,
or later dividing lines. The record does not supply a mathematical proposition, assumptions, or a
conclusion. Intake cannot silently choose a familiar result merely because it is formalizable.

## Candidate mathematical scope

A source-faithful statement in this area may require the following objects and conventions:

- a first-order language `L`, a complete consistent `L`-theory `T`, and a sufficiently saturated
  or monster model, with all universe and cardinal bounds explicit;
- formulas with object and parameter variables, finite or infinite parameter sets, indiscernible
  sequences, consistency and inconsistency of families of instances;
- the tree property (`TP1` in later terminology), dividing and forking over a base, extension and
  nonforking, and possibly Lascar strong types; and
- small-model, algebraically closed, extension-base, hyperimaginary, or elimination hypotheses,
  depending on the theorem selected from the source.

The next phase must choose one pinpoint source-labelled proposition. Plausible but inequivalent
roots include the definition of simplicity by absence of the tree property; an equivalence between
tree-property and dividing/forking formulations; symmetry, local character, or transitivity of
independence in simple theories; an independence theorem; or a classification/counting statement
for a restricted family. A definition alone must not be promoted as a theorem of classification.

## Statement-phase decisions

The statement phase must freeze the source edition and exact theorem/page, the historical versus
modern meaning of "tree property", completeness and consistency assumptions, parameter domains,
cardinal bounds, saturation, base-set conditions, binder order, degenerate theories, and the exact
conclusion. It must distinguish `TP1` from `TP2`, simplicity from stability or supersimplicity, and
real tuples from imaginaries or hyperimaginaries. Only then can it choose minimal imports, fix the
foundation and TCB profiles, elaborate a canonical expression, check transports, and run removed-
hypothesis, changed-domain, binder-scope, and boundary mutations.

## Explicit exclusions

- Replacing the record with the tautology that a theory is simple iff it satisfies a newly defined
  predicate named `Simple`.
- Assuming the desired independence or classification property as a structure field and returning
  that field as the proof.
- Substituting stability, NIP, NSOP1, supersimplicity, simplicity of groups, or the unrelated
  Sauer-Shelah lemma.
- Selecting one example of a simple theory instead of a theorem about the indicated class.
- Treating the repository's `已验证` label, a bibliographic citation, or the generic Lean probe as
  evidence for a source theorem or kernel-checked root.
