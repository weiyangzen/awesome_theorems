# Scope map

## Included theorem family

- A genuine space-time local smoothing estimate for solutions of a wave equation, or for the
  Fourier integral operator used by the selected 1991 source.
- The comparison with fixed-time Sobolev regularity, including the exact derivative improvement
  or loss and its norm convention.
- The source's spatial dimension, exponent range, time localization, frequency localization,
  support assumptions, curvature/nondegeneracy assumptions, and endpoint policy.
- Uniformity of the estimate, with every dependency of its constant exposed.

## Decisions required before statement freeze

The statement phase must independently inspect an immutable primary source and identify one exact
theorem/page. It must freeze the wave propagator or Fourier integral operator, Fourier convention,
spatial domain or manifold, dimension, scalar field, time interval, input class, cutoff and support
conditions, Sobolev/Bessel-potential spaces, `L^p` order and measure, exponent inequalities,
frequency parameter, derivative exponent, constants, and endpoint inclusion. It must also decide
whether the repository's year 1991 refers to a Sogge-only result or the Seeger-Sogge-Stein paper.

## Explicit exclusions

- The broader local smoothing conjecture or any exponent range not proved in the selected source.
- A fixed-time wave estimate, Strichartz estimate, restriction theorem, maximal estimate,
  decoupling theorem, or interpolation lemma substituted for space-time local smoothing.
- A Euclidean special case substituted for a variable-coefficient/FIO theorem, or the reverse.
- A result for a different wave equation, dimension, norm order, or endpoint without checked
  equivalence or implications covering the canonical claim.
- A structure or predicate that assumes the desired local smoothing inequality as data.
- The duplicate repository target `THM-M-1211` as evidence that this target has been accepted.
- The inventory label `已验证` as evidence of a proof.

Zero input, degenerate frequency, zero-length time intervals, endpoint exponents, and the precise
meaning of fractional differentiation remain boundary cases to map from the selected source.
