# Scope map

## Preserved source scope

- Subject: an unspecified potential from potential theory.
- Location: an unspecified boundary.
- Claimed property: boundary behavior described by the title as a jump relation.
- Historical scope: nineteenth century, attributed only to multiple mathematicians.

This is all the repository source fixes. In particular, the word "potential" does not select a
single-layer, double-layer, Newtonian, logarithmic, Cauchy, or parabolic potential.

## Decisions required before statement freeze

The statement phase must identify a primary theorem and freeze the kernel and normalization,
ambient dimension and scalar field, density space, domain and boundary regularity and orientation,
interior/exterior or nontangential traces, sign convention, almost-everywhere versus pointwise
meaning, and the exact jump/average formula. It must also freeze exceptional dimensions, boundary
points, zero density, unbounded domains, and any principal-value interpretation.

## Explicit exclusions

- Choosing the classical double-layer jump formula merely because it is the best-known reading.
- Substituting continuity of a single-layer potential, a normal-derivative jump, or a Cauchy
  integral Plemelj formula without source provenance.
- Treating an abstract structure with the desired jump relation as a proof of a concrete theorem.
- Treating the metadata label `已验证` as source or kernel evidence.
