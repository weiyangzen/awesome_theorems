# Scope map

## Included topic boundary

- Arthur's global trace-formula program for reductive algebraic groups.
- A source-selected global field, group, adelic quotient, measure normalization, and test-function
  space.
- The exact source-defined distribution and its geometric and spectral expansions.
- Truncation, convergence, central-character, regularity, and invariance hypotheses required by the
  selected identity.

## Variants requiring an explicit source decision

1. **Truncated/non-invariant formula:** equality between geometric and spectral expansions of a
   truncation-dependent distribution `J^T(f)`.
2. **Invariant trace formula:** the invariant distributions obtained after the required correction
   and induction procedures; this is not merely a notation change from the non-invariant formula.
3. **Stable trace formula:** stable distributions and endoscopic transfer, requiring substantially
   more data and hypotheses.
4. **Specialized formulas:** anisotropic, compact-quotient, local, twisted, or group-specific
   specializations, none of which may replace the general repository label without a source ruling.

The statement phase must freeze ordered binders and all conventions, including the base field,
connected reductive group, minimal Levi/parabolic data, maximal compact subgroup, Haar measures,
central quotient, test function, truncation parameter and chamber, indexing sets, weights,
intertwining operators, and the precise convergence/equality sense.

## Explicit exclusions

- Selberg's trace formula as a substitute for Arthur's generalization.
- A finite-dimensional linear-algebra trace identity or character formula.
- An equality between uninterpreted variables named "geometric side" and "spectral side".
- A structure containing the desired identity as an assumed field followed by a projection.
- A compact or anisotropic special case presented as the unrestricted reductive-group formula.
- The stable or invariant formula silently substituted for the original non-invariant formula.
- The repository label `已验证` as evidence of a source proof or kernel closure.

No canonical Lean target is frozen at intake because the repository phrase does not select one of
the variants above.
