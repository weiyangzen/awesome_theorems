# Scope map

## Preserved source scope

The repository fixes only target `THM-M-1369`, the label `KAM theory`, the collective attribution
Kolmogorov/Arnold/Moser, the year 1963, and the gloss `stability of nearly integrable Hamiltonian
systems`. Intake preserves the Hamiltonian perturbation subject and its stability theme. It does
not silently turn this field description into a quantified theorem.

## Proposition-changing decisions

An approved target correction must freeze all of the following:

- one exact theorem rather than the entire KAM theory: a fixed-frequency Kolmogorov theorem, a
  positive-measure persistence theorem, an isoenergetic result, a Moser twist-map theorem, a
  differentiable or Gevrey extension, or another precisely cited root;
- finite-dimensional action-angle coordinates or a different phase space, scalar field, action and
  angle domains, torus dimension, parameter carriers, and symplectic conventions;
- the Hamiltonian split, integrable part, perturbation, frequency map, analytic or differentiable
  function spaces, norms, complex-neighborhood widths, and derivative bounds;
- the exact Kolmogorov, twist, isoenergetic, Russmann, or other nondegeneracy condition;
- rational independence or a quantitative Diophantine condition, including integer-vector norm,
  constants, exponent bounds, exclusions, and quantifier scopes;
- the perturbation inequality and every dependency of its smallness threshold;
- the conclusion: invariant embedding or canonical transformation, conjugacy equation, frequency
  preservation or shift, regularity, closeness and uniqueness estimates, locality, and any
  positive-measure or density statement; and
- ordered binders, universes, hypotheses, logical principles, exceptional cases, proof boundary,
  and checked transports for every credited alternate encoding.

Each choice changes the proposition. This is a resolution ledger, not a canonical claim.

## Candidate roots not credited

- Kolmogorov's analytic persistence theorem for a nondegenerate conditionally periodic motion.
- Arnold's 1963 proof and near-integrable Hamiltonian formulation of Kolmogorov's theorem.
- A modern positive-measure Cantor-family theorem saying that most suitable nonresonant tori
  persist under a sufficiently small perturbation.
- Moser's differentiable invariant-curve or twist-map branch.
- Isoenergetic, lower-dimensional, reversible, Gevrey, finite-differentiability, PDE, or other KAM
  variants.

No candidate is selected, conjoined, asserted, or credited at intake.

## Degenerate and boundary cases

The statement phase must decide at least torus dimension zero or one; empty or boundary action
domains; zero, resonant, rationally dependent, or non-Diophantine frequency vectors; singular
Hessian, frequency map, or twist matrix; zero perturbation and perturbations at or beyond the
threshold; loss of analytic width or insufficient differentiability; a torus at the domain
boundary; frequency preservation versus drift; lower-dimensional or destroyed resonant tori;
multiple embeddings differing by phase; and persistence of one torus versus survival of a
positive-measure family.

No case is excluded at intake. A structure or hypothesis that stores the desired invariant torus,
conjugacy, canonical transformation, stability, or persistence conclusion as data is circular.

## Neighbor and substitution exclusions

- `THM-M-1370` owns the original Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem-family record. Its 1954 branch cannot be
  silently chosen as the whole umbrella target.
- `THM-M-1371` owns Moser's twist theorem. An invariant-curve theorem for an annulus map is not the
  whole KAM theory.
- `THM-M-1372` owns the Nekhoroshev estimate. Long but finite action stability for all allowed
  initial data is not perpetual quasi-periodic motion on persistent invariant tori.
- `THM-M-1373` owns generic Hamiltonian-system infrastructure. Hamilton's equations alone do not
  state a perturbative persistence theorem.
- `THM-M-1547` and legacy `S1-M-206` concern Liouville-Arnold integrability and action-angle
  structure. Integrable invariant tori before perturbation do not prove their persistence.
- `THM-P-0774` is a non-covered physics-catalog KAM record saying that most nonresonant tori remain
  under a small perturbation. Without an accepted identity and ownership decision, its stronger
  wording and evidence do not transfer.
- The Kolmogorov-Arnold representation or superposition theorem associated with Hilbert's
  thirteenth problem is unrelated and expressly excluded.
- Siegel linearization, Birkhoff normal form, Poincare recurrence, adiabatic invariance, Arnold
  diffusion bounds, numerical orbits, sampled frequencies, and floating-point smallness tests are
  not substitutes for a source-selected root.
- Generic analytic, Fourier, symplectic, ODE, and flow APIs are substrate, not the theorem.
- The repository's untrusted `verified` label and this intake probe supply no source or proof
  credit.

## Formal boundary

Pinned mathlib exposes analytic maps, finite unit tori and Fourier monomials, canonical symplectic
matrices, integral curves, and flows. The probe authenticates only those adjacent interfaces. It
does not define a source-specific KAM datum, analytic strip norm, Diophantine predicate, normal-form
iteration, invariant-torus conjugacy equation, or persistence theorem. No canonical Lean target,
expression fingerprint, checked transport, mutation suite, or proof body is claimed at intake.
