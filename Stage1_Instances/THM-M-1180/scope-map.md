# Scope map

## Included subject

- Real Monge-Ampere equations for convex functions on finite-dimensional Euclidean domains.
- The equation `det(D^2 u) = f`, interpreted in the classical, Alexandrov, or viscosity sense as
  fixed by the selected primary theorem.
- A genuine Caffarelli regularity conclusion, potentially strict convexity/localization, interior
  `C^{1,alpha}`, `C^{2,alpha}`, or `W^{2,p}` regularity, but only after one exact source theorem is
  selected.
- Quantitative domain, section, normalization, and density assumptions used by that theorem.

## Statement-phase decisions

The label denotes a family, so the statement phase must select exactly one primary theorem and
freeze: dimension; open/bounded/convex domain hypotheses; boundary data; convexity and solution
notion; bounds, continuity, or oscillation assumptions on `f`; normalization of sections; local or
global conclusion; regularity exponent and quantified constants; and degenerate cases. Binder order,
universes, norms, Hessian/determinant encoding, and the precise conclusion must follow that source.

## Explicit exclusions

- Replacing the nonlinear Monge-Ampere theorem by a one-dimensional ODE, a quadratic example, or a
  generic smooth-function lemma.
- Assuming the requested regularity as a hypothesis or packaging it as a structure field.
- Substituting optimal-transport regularity without proving the exact source theorem's equivalence.
- Treating an a priori estimate for already smooth solutions as the full weak-solution regularity
  theorem unless that is exactly the selected source statement.
- Using the broad repository phrase "Monge-Ampere regularity" as though it uniquely fixed a theorem.

The later formal target must expose concrete convexity, Monge-Ampere measure or Hessian determinant,
domain/section geometry, and function-space regularity interfaces, or record their exact API blockers.
