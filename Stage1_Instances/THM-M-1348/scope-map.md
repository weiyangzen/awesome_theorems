# Scope map

## Preserved theorem family

The intake preserves the planar omega-limit classification family named by the catalog. A later
statement phase may select a root only after an immutable authoritative source passage is mapped and
independently reviewed. Candidate mathematical components, none yet credited as the theorem, are:

- an open subset of the real plane, or another source-selected planar phase space;
- a sufficiently regular autonomous vector field and its source-defined local or global flow;
- a selected initial point, forward or backward orbit, and corresponding omega-limit set;
- a nonempty compact omega-limit hypothesis, or source assumptions from which it is derived;
- an exclusion of fixed points, or an explicit finite-fixed-point branch structure; and
- equality with a regular nonconstant periodic orbit, or a source-selected wider classification.

## Decisions required at statement freeze

The statement phase must freeze all of the following from an approved source rather than from a
textbook convention:

1. The exact edition, theorem or lemma locator, incorporated definitions, proof boundary, correction
   history, and independent review.
2. Whether the phase space is all of `R^2`, an open planar domain, or a two-dimensional manifold,
   and how trajectories approaching the domain boundary are treated.
3. Whether the dynamics comes from an autonomous `C1` vector field, another regularity class, a
   local flow, a global flow, or a source-defined maximal solution.
4. The time domain and direction, initial point, solution existence interval, and precise orbit and
   omega-limit definitions.
5. Whether boundedness, precompactness, containment in a compact region, nonemptiness, compactness,
   connectedness, and invariance are assumptions or derived facts.
6. Whether fixed points are excluded, merely finite, isolated, or represented as a separate branch.
7. Whether the conclusion is equality with one regular periodic orbit, a fixed-orbit alternative,
   or a finite family of fixed points plus nonclosed connecting orbits.
8. The meanings of fixed point, regular point, periodic orbit, nonconstant orbit, positive or
   minimal period, and equality of orbit ranges.
9. The exact ordered binders, quantifier dependencies, hypotheses, conclusion, and logic strength.

## Degenerate and boundary cases

Source review must explicitly dispose of a stationary initial point; an omega-limit set containing
one or more equilibria; empty or noncompact omega-limit behavior; a forward solution that is not
global; an orbit leaving the planar domain; equilibrium trajectories counted as periodic; zero or
nonminimal periods; self-intersecting parametrizations; and the distinction between convergence to
a point or cycle and equality of the omega-limit set with that object.
