# Scope map

## Received claim

The repository names the Goldschmidt theorem and supplies only "a basic result in fusion-system
theory." This establishes a topic and attribution, not a proposition. The phrase could point to a
classical finite-group fusion result, the Alperin-Goldschmidt fusion theorem, a result inside
Goldschmidt's *2-Fusion in Finite Groups*, the 1975 strongly-closed-subgroup theorem matching the
catalog year, or another eponymous theorem. Intake preserves this ambiguity instead of silently
choosing the best-known modern formulation.

## Candidate family boundary

A modern statement witness describes the Alperin-Goldschmidt theorem for a saturated fusion system
`F` on a finite `p`-group `S`: `F` is generated by the `F`-automorphisms of `S` and of its
`F`-essential subgroups, equivalently suitable morphisms factor into restrictions of those
automorphisms. This is a plausible family, not the canonical target. Selecting it would still
require all of the following source-mapped choices:

- whether the source is about conjugation fusion in a finite ambient group or an abstract
  saturated fusion system;
- the prime, finite `p`-group, objects, morphisms, saturation axioms, and universe levels;
- the exact definition of `F`-essential subgroup and its conjugacy representatives;
- generation equality versus a morphism-factorization conclusion, including composition order,
  restrictions, intermediate subgroups, and whether inverses are explicit;
- whether `Aut_F(S)` is included separately and whether the indexing family includes `S`;
- empty-family, trivial-group, abelian, nilpotent, constrained, and group-realizable cases; and
- which clauses are theorem statement, incorporated definitions, or proof architecture.

## Related but nonidentical results

- Alperin's fusion theorem and the Alperin-Goldschmidt refinement are related theorem families;
  neither name alone establishes the intended source boundary.
- The focal subgroup theorem records the intersection of a Sylow subgroup with the ambient
  commutator subgroup. It is fusion-related finite-group substrate, not a generation or
  factorization theorem for a fusion system.
- Burnside transfer and normal-complement results can be ingredients in finite-group arguments but
  do not state the Goldschmidt root.
- Thompson transfer (`THM-M-0072`) is a separate catalog target and supplies no source, scope, or
  proof credit here.

## Explicit exclusions

- A generic statement saying only that fusion is "controlled" or "generated" without defining
  the controlling objects and equality/factorization relation.
- The focal subgroup theorem, Burnside transfer, Sylow conjugacy, or Alperin's theorem silently
  substituted for a source-selected Goldschmidt proposition.
- A finite-group-only special case substituted for an abstract saturated-fusion-system theorem, or
  the converse, without a checked source relationship.
- A structure, hypothesis, or definition that stores the desired factorization or generation
  conclusion.
- The catalog's `已验证` label, a theorem name, bibliographic metadata, search absence, or a
  successful `#check` treated as source or proof evidence.

No canonical Lean expression, ordered binders, hypotheses, conclusion, alternate transport, or
degenerate-case exclusion is frozen during intake.
