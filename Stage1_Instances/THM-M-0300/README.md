atomic decomposition." The repository supplies only the gloss "atomic decomposition of the
`H^1` space," attributes it to Charles Fefferman and Elias Stein in 1972, and carries an untrusted
`verified` label. It does not identify a source theorem, define `H^1` or an atom, or state the
representation, convergence, or norm comparison.

The same authors, year, gloss, importance, and status occur under the separately scheduled
`THM-M-0362`, "Atomic decomposition theorem." That is a probable catalog duplicate, not an
accepted source, dependency, or evidence donor. Only the integration lane may decide identity and
canonical ownership.

Several non-interchangeable results fit the gloss: real Hardy `H^1(R^n)`, analytic or boundary
Hardy spaces, and Hardy spaces on more general measure spaces. Even on `R^n`, atom conventions
differ in support shape, cancellation, size norm, scalar field, convergence mode, and whether the
conclusion includes both directions and quantitative norm equivalence. Selecting one without an
inspected source would replace the received target with invented mathematics.

The intake therefore freezes the ambiguity, duplicate boundary, and prohibited substitutions
rather than a proposition. Its provisional vector is `[H5, M4, R4]`: `H5` applies only to the
received catalog wording, which is not yet one stable proposition; it does not say that a corrected,
source-selected atomic-decomposition theorem is false or mathematically open. No usable exact
formal artifact or source-faithful proof reconstruction is credited.
