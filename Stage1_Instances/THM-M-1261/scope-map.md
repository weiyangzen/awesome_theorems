# Scope map

## Included family

- Fourier integral operators represented locally by oscillatory integrals with a phase and
  amplitude/symbol, subject to the selected source's homogeneity and nondegeneracy conditions.
- One theorem connecting that operator class to hyperbolic PDE, but only after a primary numbered
  result is selected. Plausible branches include construction of solution parametrices,
  canonical-relation mapping, composition, or Sobolev boundedness; they are alternatives, not one
  combined claim.
- All domain/manifold, bundle, support/properness, order, regularity, and wave-front assumptions
  that the chosen result uses.

## Decisions required by statement phase

Select an immutable edition of a primary source and a theorem/page, then fix the Fourier-transform
normalization, real or complex scalar field, manifolds and dimensions, phase equivalence,
amplitude class and order, canonical relation, proper-support conditions, function/distribution
spaces, quantifier order, constant dependencies, and the exact PDE conclusion. Determine whether
the result is local, microlocal, or global and whether equality is exact or modulo smoothing
operators.

## Exclusions

- A definition or historical description presented as a theorem.
- A generic Fourier transform, convolution, or pseudodifferential-operator fact substituted for an
  FIO result.
- A special phase or Euclidean multiplier presented as the general manifold theorem without a
  checked specialization relationship.
- A theorem that assumes the desired mapping or parametrix conclusion as an opaque structure
  field.
- Any unproved endpoint, global extension, or stronger symbol class absent from the selected
  source.

Zero amplitudes, empty supports, degenerate phases, dimension-zero cases, and smoothing remainders
must be treated explicitly in the eventual statement and mutation tests.
