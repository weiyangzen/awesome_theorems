# Scope map

## Preserved theorem family

The intake preserves the 1954 Kolmogorov branch of KAM theory named by the catalog: persistence of
conditionally or quasi-periodic invariant motions under a sufficiently small perturbation of an
integrable Hamiltonian. A later statement phase may select an exact root only from an immutable,
independently reviewed source passage. Candidate components, none yet credited as the theorem,
include:

- a real-analytic Hamiltonian in action-angle coordinates near a finite-dimensional invariant
  torus;
- a nondegenerate integrable part, expressed by an invertible Hessian, frequency map, or another
  source-specific twist condition;
- a nonresonant or quantitatively Diophantine frequency vector;
- a source-specific analytic norm, complex neighborhood, and perturbation-smallness threshold;
- a near-identity canonical transformation or embedding of an invariant torus;
- quasi-periodic motion on the surviving torus, with a specified, adjusted, or selected frequency;
  and
- a local persistence statement for one torus or a measure statement for a family of tori.

## Decisions required at statement freeze

1. Preserve one lawful complete source edition, select a precisely delimited result, map every
   incorporated definition and proof boundary, audit translation and corrections, and obtain
   independent source approval.
2. Reconcile the catalog's 1954 date and combined Kolmogorov-Arnold attribution with Kolmogorov's
   original announcement and Arnold's later proof/formulation. Do not merge their contracts.
3. Fix the Hamiltonian form, scalar field, phase space, action and angle domains, torus dimension,
   parameter domains, and symplectic or canonical-coordinate conventions.
4. Fix the regularity category and norm: real analytic, finite differentiability, Gevrey, or
   another source-defined class, including the complex-neighborhood widths when applicable.
5. State the integrable part, perturbation, frequency map, and exact Kolmogorov nondegeneracy or
   twist hypothesis, including where derivatives and determinants are evaluated.
6. State the arithmetic hypothesis with all constants, norms, integer-vector exclusions, and
   exponent bounds. "Nonresonant" and "Diophantine" are not interchangeable without a source.
7. State the perturbation bound and dependency of its threshold on dimension, analytic widths,
   nondegeneracy, and arithmetic constants; decide whether existence is qualitative or quantitative.
8. Fix the conclusion: invariant embedding or canonical transform, conjugacy equation, frequency
   preservation or shift, closeness estimates, uniqueness, locality, and any positive-measure or
   density assertion.
9. Freeze ordered binders, quantifier dependencies, exceptional and boundary cases, logical
   principles, and checked transports for every credited alternate encoding.

## Degenerate and boundary cases

Source review must explicitly dispose of torus dimension zero or one; an empty action domain;
constant or resonant frequencies; failure of the source's Diophantine inequalities; singular
frequency-map Hessian or twist matrix; zero perturbation; perturbations at or beyond the threshold;
loss of analyticity width; a torus at the boundary of the action domain; frequency drift; destroyed
resonant tori; lower-dimensional tori; multiple embeddings differing by phase; and the distinction
between persistence of one torus and survival of a large-measure Cantor family.

No case is excluded at intake. A statement that assumes an invariant torus, conjugacy, canonical
transformation, or persistence conclusion as data is circular rather than a KAM theorem.

## Neighbor and substitution exclusions

- `THM-M-1369` owns the broader catalog entry "KAM theory." Its stability slogan and any future
  evidence do not select or close this original-form target.
- `THM-M-1371` owns Moser's twist theorem; an invariant-curve result for an annulus map is not
  silently interchangeable with Kolmogorov's Hamiltonian invariant-torus theorem.
- `THM-M-1372` owns Nekhoroshev estimates, which concern long but finite stability times rather than
  invariant-torus persistence.
- `THM-M-1373` owns the general Hamiltonian-systems entry; Hamilton's equations alone do not prove
  a perturbation theorem.
- `THM-M-1547` and legacy `S1-M-206` concern Liouville-Arnold integrability and action-angle
  structure. Their abstract proposition fields and checked symplectic/ODE substrate do not prove
  persistence under perturbation.
- The physics-catalog KAM item `THM-P-0774` is outside the rev-5.6 mathematical target set and may
  serve only as disambiguating metadata, never as a substituted target or shared evidence.
- The Kolmogorov-Arnold representation or superposition theorem associated with Hilbert's
  thirteenth problem is expressly excluded despite the similar English name.
- Siegel linearization, Birkhoff normal form, Poincare recurrence, adiabatic invariance, numerical
  orbit plots, and finite-time near-stability are not substitutes for the exact source-selected
  KAM claim.
- Generic analytic, Fourier, symplectic, ODE, and flow APIs are substrate, not the theorem.
- The repository's `verified` label and this intake probe supply no source or proof credit.

## Formal boundary

Pinned mathlib exposes real and complex analytic maps, finite products of the unit additive circle
and Fourier series on them, canonical symplectic matrices, ODE integral curves, and flows. The probe
authenticates only these adjacent interfaces. It does not define a Hamiltonian KAM datum, a
Diophantine frequency predicate in the KAM sense, analytic strip norms, a canonical transformation,
an invariant-torus conjugacy equation, or a perturbative persistence theorem. No canonical Lean
target, expression fingerprint, checked transport, mutation suite, or proof body is claimed at
intake.
