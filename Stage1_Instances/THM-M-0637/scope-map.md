# Scope map

## Included theorem family

The repository wording and inspected primary-source lead identify this candidate family without yet
freezing it as the canonical target:

- an ambient real Banach space, represented in Lean by a real normed space with completeness;
- a closed convex subset `H`, with nonemptiness requiring an explicit source/convention decision;
- a continuous self-map `F` of `H`;
- compactness of the particular image `F(H)`;
- existence, but not uniqueness, of a point of `H` fixed by `F`.

`Satz II` on printed page 175 of the inspected 1930 scan is the principal source lead. The source
derives it from a compact-domain result (`Satz I`) by passing to the least closed convex set
generated by the compact image. This proof relationship does not make the two statements identical.

## Proposition-changing decisions

The statement phase must resolve all of the following against an accepted source crosswalk:

- whether nonemptiness is explicit or an incorporated convention of the source;
- an ambient function with `ContinuousOn` and `Set.MapsTo` versus a continuous subtype self-map;
- global continuity versus continuity only on `H`;
- literal compactness of `F '' H` versus compactness of its closure (relative compactness);
- compactness of this one image versus the modern condition that every bounded set has relatively
  compact image;
- the source's real `B`-space convention and the exact modern Banach-space typeclasses;
- empty and singleton domains, constant maps, unbounded closed domains, and already compact domains;
- the intended distinctness, aliasing, or merger of `THM-M-0637` and `THM-M-0318`.

Each change is potentially proposition-changing. It needs an approved source decision and, for any
alternate encoding, a checked Lean transport rather than prose equivalence.

## Explicit exclusions

- `THM-M-0318`'s compact-convex-domain formulation copied without an identity decision and checked
  relationship to the compact-image form.
- Brouwer's finite-dimensional theorem, Tychonoff's locally convex theorem, Kakutani's set-valued
  theorem, Banach's contraction theorem, or Markov-Kakutani as the terminal claim.
- A linear compact-operator theorem, a Schauder basis theorem, or a Schauder PDE estimate.
- A weakened result on one finite-dimensional space, a contraction-only special case, or a theorem
  whose hypotheses contain the desired fixed point.
- The catalog label `verified`, a citation, an API probe, or a foreign target's state treated as
  source fidelity or proof credit.

## Formal surface discovered at intake

Pinned mathlib exposes `CompleteSpace`, `Set.Nonempty`, `IsClosed`, `Convex`, `ContinuousOn`,
`Set.MapsTo`, `IsCompact`, `IsCompactOperator`, and `Function.IsFixedPt`. `IntakeProbe.lean` checks
these names under discovery imports only. `IsCompactOperator` is designed for continuous linear or
semilinear maps and its neighborhood/image characterization does not select the source's nonlinear
domain-relative premise. No exact theorem, exhaustive anchor inventory, or proof closure is
credited at intake.
