# Scope map

## Included subject

- Littlewood-Paley frequency decompositions on an explicitly fixed domain such as `R^n` or a
  torus, using stated Fourier-transform and dyadic-cutoff conventions.
- A substantive theorem such as resolution/convergence of a dyadic decomposition or a
  square-function norm equivalence/inequality for an explicit function space and exponent range.
- Homogeneous versus inhomogeneous forms, low-frequency terms, quotient-by-polynomials issues,
  endpoints, constants, and almost-everywhere/distribution/norm convergence only as stated by the
  selected source.

## Statement-phase decisions

The next phase must select one theorem from an inspected primary source. It must freeze scalar
field, dimension and domain, Fourier normalization, cutoff support and partition properties,
dyadic operators, admissible functions or distributions, exponent ranges, equality or norm
comparison, quantification of constants, convergence mode, and boundary cases. None follows from
the label "Littlewood-Paley theory."

## Explicit exclusions

- Treating a definition of dyadic blocks, or the tautological reconstruction supplied as a field
  of an assumed decomposition package, as the target theorem.
- Substituting a generic Fourier inversion, Plancherel, Sobolev, Besov, or martingale theorem.
- Selecting a convenient square-function inequality without a source-statement crosswalk.
- Assuming the partition of unity, multiplier estimates, or convergence that constitutes the
  mathematical content and then crediting a projection from those assumptions as proof.

The formal target remains unset until exact source selection resolves these choices.
