# Scope map

## Preserved repository scope

The repository fixes target `THM-M-0969`, the title `Lovász局部引理`, the attribution Laszlo
Lovasz, the year 1975, and the gloss `稀疏依赖事件同时不发生的概率`. Importance `高` and status
`已验证` are inventory metadata, not human-source or kernel evidence. Intake preserves only the
Lovasz-local-lemma family identified by these words.

The usual symmetric finite lemma is a scope locator, not the selected target. The gloss is equally
compatible with asymmetric variable bounds, a product lower bound, lopsided dependency, or an
infinite compactness extension.

## Proposition-changing decisions

An approved statement phase must freeze all of the following from a pinpoint immutable source:

1. Whether the root is the symmetric, asymmetric/general, lopsided, variable, cluster-expansion,
   or another named local-lemma formulation.
2. Whether the event family is finite, countable, or arbitrary, and every finiteness, local
   finiteness, compactness, measurability, and probability-space assumption.
3. The dependency structure: directed relation or simple graph, whether self-neighbors are
   included, the exact maximum-degree convention, and how missing or repeated vertices behave.
4. The semantic dependency hypothesis: mutual independence from the sigma-algebra generated by
   all outside-neighborhood events, a conditional-probability bound, lopsided nonpositive
   correlation, or another source-defined condition. Pairwise independence is not interchangeable.
5. The numeric hypotheses and codomains: a common `p` and degree `d`, weights `x_i`, a condition
   such as `e * p * (d + 1) <= 1`, or another constant/product criterion, including strict versus
   non-strict endpoints and `Real` versus `ENNReal` coercions.
6. The conclusion: positivity of the measure of the intersection of complements, nonemptiness,
   an explicit product lower bound, or a finite satisfiability/existence statement.
7. Ordered binders, universes, typeclass assumptions, complement/intersection encodings, graph
   conventions, and all empty-family, zero-degree, zero-probability, and endpoint cases.

These choices produce inequivalent propositions. The list is a resolution ledger, not a theorem.

## Candidate branches not credited

- A finite symmetric lemma for measurable events, probability at most `p`, and dependency degree
  at most `d`, concluding positive probability under a source-selected inequality.
- A finite asymmetric lemma with weights `x_i` and bounds of the form
  `P(A_i) <= x_i * product (1 - x_j)` over source-selected neighbors.
- A lopsided lemma with source-defined conditional-probability or negative-dependency semantics
  rather than ordinary mutual independence.
- An infinite extension derived from all finite subfamilies plus an explicit compactness or
  continuity hypothesis.

No candidate is canonical, asserted, or credited at intake.

## Explicit exclusions and neighbors

Pairwise-independent events, a union-bound argument, or the fully independent special case cannot
replace sparse dependency. A dependency structure or hypothesis that stores the desired positive
intersection conclusion cannot serve as its proof. A result about one event or an empty family is
not the general root.

`THM-M-0970` (Moser-Tardos) is the separate constructive/algorithmic target, and `THM-M-0971`
(Shearer's bound) is a separate optimal-condition target. Neither may substitute for this root or
transfer source, proof, or completion credit. The untrusted `已验证` label and adjacent Lean APIs
also confer no source or proof credit.

## Boundary cases to resolve

- Empty and singleton index types, empty sample space, and zero or non-probability measures.
- Degree `d = 0`, self-loops, asymmetric adjacency, duplicate events, and isolated vertices.
- `p = 0`, `p = 1`, weights `x_i = 0` or `1`, strict versus weak inequalities, and equality at the
  selected numeric threshold.
- Empty intersections, finite versus infinite intersections, measurability of those intersections,
  positivity in `ENNReal` versus `Real`, and nonempty-set versus positive-measure conclusions.
- Conditional probabilities on null conditioning events and the exact sigma-algebra generated by
  events outside a neighborhood.

No case is excluded before the exact proposition is source-selected.
