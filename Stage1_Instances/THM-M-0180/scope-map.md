# Scope map

## Preserved repository scope

- Attribution: Shiu-Yuen Cheng and Shing-Tung Yau.
- Date: 1975.
- Subject area: differential geometry.
- Title branch: a Cheng-Yau maximum principle.
- Gloss branch: a gradient estimate for eigenfunctions.

The title and gloss are retained as two unresolved branches, not merged into an invented claim.

## Decisions required before statement freeze

Primary-source inspection must decide which branch the target owns. For a maximum-principle branch,
it must freeze completeness, dimension, curvature lower bounds, regularity and boundedness of the
function, whether the conclusion uses an approximating sequence, and the gradient and Laplacian
limits/signs. For an eigenfunction-estimate branch, it must freeze the eigenvalue equation and sign
convention, manifold/domain geometry, curvature and boundary hypotheses, positivity or nodal
restrictions, local/global region, norm, constant dependencies, and the exact pointwise inequality.

The selected source must also decide disconnected and zero-dimensional manifolds, constant
functions, attained suprema, zero eigenvalue, empty domains, manifolds with boundary, and whether
compactness is a special case. Universes and binder order follow the source rather than convenience.

## Explicit exclusions

- Substituting the Omori-Yau maximum principle, a positive-harmonic-function gradient estimate, or
  a Laplace eigenfunction estimate until a primary source resolves the repository's identity.
- Combining hypotheses and conclusions from different Cheng-Yau results into a hybrid theorem.
- Replacing the quantitative claim with compact-space attainment of a maximum.
- Treating a structure or hypothesis containing the requested gradient/Laplacian conclusion as its
  proof, or treating `已验证` as evidence.
