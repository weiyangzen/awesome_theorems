# Source-statement crosswalk

## Candidate primary sources

- Andreas Floer, "Morse theory for Lagrangian intersections", *Journal of Differential Geometry*
  **28** (1988), 513-547, DOI `10.4310/jdg/1214442477`. This is the principal discovery anchor
  for the original Lagrangian-intersection construction and Hamiltonian-isotopy intersection
  estimates. The statement phase must inspect a stable copy and record the exact theorem/page,
  incorporated definitions, and hypotheses; this intake has not performed that pinpoint audit.
- Andreas Floer, "Cuplength estimates on Lagrangian intersections", *Communications on Pure and
  Applied Mathematics* **42** (1989), 335-356, DOI
  `10.1002/cpa.3160420402`. This is a primary anchor for the distinct cup-length estimate and must
  not be conflated with the total-Betti-number form.
- Andreas Floer, "Witten's complex and infinite-dimensional Morse theory", *Journal of
  Differential Geometry* **30** (1989), 207-221, DOI `10.4310/jdg/1214443291`. This is useful
  primary context for the Floer-complex/Morse-theoretic mechanism, but does not by itself select
  the canonical theorem for this metadata entry.

These are discovery anchors only. They have not been archived and hashed as immutable source
artifacts, checked for corrections, crosswalked at theorem/page granularity, or independently
reviewed. They therefore establish neither H0 nor any machine status.

## Required statement crosswalk

Before the statement phase can freeze a canonical claim, a source reviewer must:

1. pin a stable edition of the chosen primary paper and record the exact theorem, pages, definitions,
   and notation incorporated into the claim;
2. enumerate the compactness, Hamiltonian-isotopy, transversality, relative-homotopy, Maslov,
   orientability, coefficient, and grading assumptions actually present;
3. distinguish construction of the complex, the identity `d^2 = 0`, continuation/invariance,
   comparison with ordinary/Morse homology, and the intersection inequality;
4. distinguish the total-Betti-number and cup-length conclusions and their coefficient systems;
5. record corrections, later changes of conventions, and whether any modern reformulation is
   equivalent to or stronger than the original source statement; and
6. obtain independent approval of the source-to-canonical-statement mapping.

The Lean crosswalk must then map each symplectic, Lagrangian, Hamiltonian, analytic, moduli-space,
and homological object to an exact type or predicate. Missing pseudoholomorphic-curve
infrastructure must be recorded rather than replaced by axioms or an abstract surrogate. Until
that work is complete, there is no canonical formal target or statement fingerprint.
