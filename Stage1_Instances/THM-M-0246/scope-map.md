## Candidate family A: F. and M. Riesz theorem

The title and 1916 date suggest a boundary-value theorem of the brothers Frigyes (F.) and Marcel
(M.) Riesz. A standard modern analytic-measure form says that a finite complex Borel measure on the
unit circle whose Fourier coefficients vanish on one half-line is absolutely continuous with
respect to Haar/Lebesgue measure. A historically closer formulation may instead concern a Cauchy
integral or bounded analytic function and boundary behavior. These formulations cannot be treated
as identical without a source-approved bridge.

Statement work would have to freeze the circle model and measure normalization, finite complex
measure representation, Fourier-coefficient sign and index convention, which half-line vanishes,
the exact absolute-continuity direction, and any equivalence to an analytic boundary-value form.

## Candidate family B: Marcel Riesz conjugate-function theorem

The gloss and sole attribution suggest the later strong-type theorem for the periodic conjugate
function, or a checked equivalent Hilbert-transform formulation: for `1 < p < infinity`, the
selected transform maps the selected `L^p` space boundedly to itself. Statement work would have to
freeze the periodic circle or real line, scalar field, Haar/Lebesgue normalization, exponent type
and endpoints, kernel sign and factor, Fourier multiplier and zero-mode convention, principal-value
or extension construction, and the exact constant dependence.
