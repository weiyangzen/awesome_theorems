# Scope map

## Included subject boundary

- A number field (often totally real), a quaternion algebra with explicitly stated split and
  ramified places, and an order/compact-open level subgroup.
- The associated quotient of the appropriate symmetric domain by an arithmetic group.
- The particular source theorem's conclusion: possible candidates include algebraicity/canonical
  model, representability of a moduli problem, properness/smoothness, or a uniformization result.
- Boundary choices that must be frozen include split versus division algebra, compact versus
  noncompact quotient, coarse scheme versus stack, connected component, level neatness, and bad
  reduction primes.

## Blocking source decision

Repository metadata supplies only `四元数代数上的模曲线` and an untrusted `已验证` label. It does not
identify a theorem, edition, page, or hypotheses. "Shimura curve" can denote several inequivalent
objects and results. The statement phase must select an exact primary-source theorem and preserve
its quantifiers and conclusion. Until then there is no canonical claim to elaborate.

## Explicit exclusions

- Merely defining a quaternion algebra, arithmetic group, quotient, or scheme predicate.
- Assuming a representing object and projecting properties already stored in that assumption.
- Replacing the requested result by existence of an arbitrary smooth proper relative curve.
- Treating a classical modular curve (`B = M₂(Q)`) or a special zero-dimensional case as the root.
- Crediting the legacy file, source label, or mathlib substrate anchors as theorem closure.

The next phase must freeze universes, ordered binders, degeneracies, exact source wording, Lean
declaration/expression, minimal imports, environment fingerprint, and checked transports.
