# Scope map

## Preserved source scope

The repository fixes only the Chinese label `Lyapunov指数`, the gloss `轨道分离的指数率`
("exponential rate of orbit separation"), the attribution Aleksandr Lyapunov, the year 1892, and
an untrusted `已验证` status. It supplies no primary source, theorem locator, definition, premise,
or conclusion. The intake therefore preserves only a dynamical growth-rate topic boundary.

## Proposition-changing decisions

An approved statement correction must obtain an exact source and freeze all of the following:

- a discrete self-map, a differentiable flow, or a linear/random cocycle, including the time
  domain, time direction, invertibility, regularity, and invariant domain;
- the phase space and any metric, normed-vector, smooth-manifold, bundle, measurable, probability,
  compactness, or finite-dimensional structure required by the source;
- a base point or orbit, a second point and limiting perturbation procedure, or a nonzero tangent
  or cocycle direction, with all quantifiers and exceptional sets;
- metric separation versus derivative/cocycle norm growth, and maximal, directional, indexed, or
  full-spectrum exponent;
- ordinary limit versus limsup/liminf, real versus extended-real codomain, logarithm base,
  normalization, norm or metric, and whether equivalent choices are theorem conclusions;
- pointwise, uniform, or almost-everywhere scope, including the measure and any ergodicity or
  integrability assumptions; and
- one truth-valued conclusion, such as existence, invariance, constancy, stability, splitting, or
  an entropy relation, with every ordered binder, premise, and boundary case.

These choices yield inequivalent propositions. They are a resolution checklist, not a canonical
claim.

## Candidate families not credited

- The upper metric separation rate of two source-specified orbits.
- The directional asymptotic growth of `D(f^[n]) x` applied to a nonzero tangent vector.
- A continuous-time exponent derived from a differentiable flow.
- A norm-growth exponent of a deterministic or random matrix or linear cocycle.
- Existence or invariance of a maximal exponent or complete spectrum.
- Almost-everywhere exponents and a measurable invariant splitting under integrability hypotheses.
- Stability or entropy consequences under sign or summability hypotheses.

No family in this list is selected or credited at intake.

## Explicit exclusions

The intake must not silently replace the target with Oseledets' multiplicative ergodic theorem
(`THM-M-1419`), Pesin theory (`THM-M-1420`), or the Pesin entropy formula (`THM-M-1421`). The
separately cataloged random-matrix Oseledets theorem (`THM-M-1056`) is also a distinct root.
Hyperbolic systems, Anosov maps, stable-manifold results, topological or measure entropy, and
stability results that merely use an exponent are not interchangeable with this target.

Likewise excluded are the Lyapunov direct method or Lyapunov functions, a numerical finite-time
exponent estimate, a plotted orbit-separation experiment, a one-dimensional toy calculation, a
definition repackaged as a theorem, and a structure that assumes the requested limit, spectrum,
or consequence as a field. None can identify or close the catalog topic.

## Degenerate and boundary scope

An exact source must decide coincident points, zero tangent vectors, zero norms or derivatives,
`log 0`, infinite exponents, failure of an ordinary limit, fixed or periodic orbits, time zero,
forward versus two-sided time, norm dependence, exceptional null sets, and nonergodic base systems.
Silently excluding these cases could strengthen or weaken the selected theorem.

## Formal boundary

No canonical Lean expression is frozen. Pinned mathlib provides adjacent iteration, metric,
derivative, logarithm, norm, and limsup APIs. The intake-only bounded name search found no
Lyapunov- or Oseledets-named declaration in pinned mathlib. These facts show possible substrate
only; they are not an anchor audit, statement elaboration, or machine-proof evidence.
