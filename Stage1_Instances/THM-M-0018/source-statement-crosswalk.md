## Inspected source leads

The best title-and-date primary lead for the unrestricted characterization is Emil Artin and Otto
Schreier, "Eine Kennzeichnung der reell abgeschlossenen Korper," *Abhandlungen aus dem
Mathematischen Seminar der Universitat Hamburg* 5 (1927), 225-231, DOI `10.1007/BF02952522`.
Reliable secondary triangulation points to its `Satz 4`. An earlier related primary publication is
"Algebraische Konstruktion reeller Korper," volume 5 (1927), 85-99, DOI
`10.1007/BF02952512`; Crossref metadata for that paper was inspected, and the secondary
triangulation identifies its `Satz 4` as a characteristic-zero restricted predecessor. The
access-controlled primary theorem passages were not directly inspected in this run, so neither
pinpoint is credited as a transcribed statement, assumption map, proof boundary, or correction
audit.

The Encyclopedia of Mathematics entry "Real closed field" (revision `oldid=48449`) is an
authoritative secondary disambiguation lead. It attributes to Artin and Schreier the
characterization of real closed fields as fields whose algebraic closure is a nontrivial finite
extension, necessarily of degree two and obtained by adjoining a square root of `-1`. It separately
states existence and order-preserving uniqueness of real closures. Its reference [a1] groups three
different 1927 Hamburg papers and page ranges (85-99, 100-115, and 225-231), reinforcing that the
catalog's broad gloss does not identify one source proposition.

The Encyclopedia's separate "Artin-Schreier theorem" entry (`oldid=54480`) primarily describes the
characteristic-`p` extension theorem and notes that the same name is also used for the criterion
that a field is orderable/formally real exactly when `-1` is not a sum of squares. That namesake is
useful disambiguation evidence, not authority to replace the catalog's real-closed-field scope.

Keith Conrad's secondary note *The Artin-Schreier Theorem*, Theorem 3.1 on pages 2-4, gives a
readable finite-algebraic-closure statement and explicitly maps its characteristic-zero and
unrestricted histories to `Satz 4` in the two papers above. It strengthens the primary-source lead
but cannot substitute for direct primary inspection, correction review, independent approval, or
the catalog's missing choice among proposition-changing variants.

These are source-family leads only. No immutable primary theorem passage, errata record, or
independent review has been accepted, so none reaches `H0`.
