# Scope map

## Provisional subject boundary

- One-dimensional commutative formal group laws or formal groups over a coefficient ring.
- A generalized multiplicative cohomology theory carrying a complex orientation.
- A construction associating a formal group law to the cohomology theory, and possibly a converse
  realization or classification statement if and only if the selected source actually asserts it.

These bullets identify a search domain, not a frozen theorem. In particular, "correspondence" could
mean a construction, an equivalence after extra hypotheses, or only a historical relationship.

## Decisions required before statement freeze

The next phase must identify a primary source and fix: the meaning of cohomology theory; whether it
is represented by a ring spectrum; complex orientation and grading conventions; the coefficient
ring; the dimension and commutativity of the formal group; coordinate dependence; strict versus
non-strict isomorphisms; functoriality; and whether the conclusion is a formal-group-law
construction, a classification, or a realization theorem. It must also resolve whether `1974` is a
publication date or unreliable inventory metadata.

## Explicit exclusions

- Quillen's theorem about complex cobordism or the Lazard ring merely renamed as Manin's result.
- The Landweber exact functor theorem, chromatic classification, or elliptic cohomology as a
  substitute for the unidentified claim.
- Manin's theorem on cubic surfaces (`THM-M-0145`) or the Manin-Drinfeld theorem.
- An abstract structure that contains the desired correspondence as an assumed field.
- The inventory label `已验证` as human-source or kernel evidence.

No Lean declaration is selected at intake. If primary-source research cannot disambiguate the
label, statement work must remain blocked rather than broaden the target to an arbitrary theorem
about formal groups.
