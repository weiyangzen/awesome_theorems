# Scope map

## Preserved theorem family

The intake preserves the literal catalog claim: existence of the Monster group, attributed to
Robert Griess in 1982. It does not silently turn that gloss into the classification of finite
simple groups, the uniqueness or recognition of the Monster, Monstrous Moonshine, or the existence
of an algebra or representation without the required group conclusion.

A later statement phase may select one exact root only after an immutable source passage is
preserved and independently mapped. Candidate components to resolve, none yet credited as the
canonical theorem, include:

- a type `G` carrying a group structure and finiteness evidence;
- nontrivial simplicity, with the source-selected convention for a finite simple group;
- the exact Monster order
  `808017424794512875886459904961710757005754368000000000` or its prime factorization;
- construction through the automorphism group of a source-defined 196883-dimensional commutative
  nonassociative algebra, if that construction is part of the selected theorem rather than proof
  data; and
- any uniqueness, recognition, automorphism, or representation conclusion actually asserted by
  the selected source passage.

## Decisions required at statement freeze

The statement phase must freeze all of the following from approved source evidence:

1. The complete source edition, exact theorem or construction locator, incorporated definitions,
   premise and conclusion boundary, corrections or errata, and independent review.
2. Whether the root is pure existence, existence of a simple group of the exact order, construction
   as an automorphism group, or a conjunction containing additional properties.
3. Whether the name "Monster" is defined by a concrete constructed object, by exact structural
   properties, by a recognition predicate, or only after a uniqueness theorem.
4. The group carrier, universe, `Group`, `Finite` or `Fintype`, nontriviality, and simplicity
   conventions, plus the exact equivalence relation used for identity up to isomorphism.
5. The exact decimal order and prime-factor product, their checked equality, and whether both are
   statement content or one is supporting evidence.
6. If an algebraic construction is in scope: the scalar field, dimension convention, multiplication,
   invariant form or distinguished elements, automorphism definition, faithfulness, and the bridge
   from the constructed automorphism group to all claimed Monster properties.
7. Whether uniqueness up to group isomorphism, completeness of the automorphism group, or
   nonabelianness is asserted, derived, or outside the target.
8. All ordered binders, hypotheses, conclusions, foundations, classical choices, computations,
   certificate boundaries, and trusted inputs.

## Degenerate and boundary cases

The exact target must prevent a trivial group, an arbitrary simple group, or an arbitrary group of
the right order from being renamed "the Monster" without the source-selected construction or
recognition data. It must settle whether simplicity already entails nontriviality, whether the order
is a hypothesis or a proved property, whether `Finite` or `Fintype` is canonical, and whether two
constructions are identified by `MulEquiv`, equality of structured objects, or another checked
transport. If an algebra is used, zero-dimensional, associative, degenerate-form, or artificially
specified automorphism-group cases must not satisfy the intended construction by accident.

## Explicit substitutions excluded

- `THM-M-0071`, classification of finite simple groups, cannot supply this existence root; this
  target is at most one sporadic branch of that classification project.
- Thompson's recognition or uniqueness results do not by themselves construct the group.
- The existence of the Griess algebra, a 196883-dimensional module, a character table, the order
  integer, or a named constant does not by itself prove existence of the required simple group.
- The order of the Monster cannot be replaced by a smaller sporadic group or by an arbitrary finite
  group with an assumed `IsMonster` field.
- Monstrous Moonshine, the moonshine module, vertex operator algebras, and Borcherds' theorem are
  related subjects, not interchangeable with the 1982 existence construction.
- A Cayley-table database, executable computation, unchecked certificate, or group name is not a
  kernel proof unless its full construction and correctness enter an accepted validation closure.
- The catalog's `已验证` label, a DOI, a successful `#check`, or an upstream theorem name supplies
  neither source acceptance nor machine-proof credit.

No canonical Lean target, expression fingerprint, alternate encoding, obligation registry, or
proof state is frozen during intake.
