## FS-N3 — cup–cap upper theorem

**Hypotheses.** Fix `n ≥ 3` and a finite planar set in general position. Choose a direction not orthogonal to any pair difference, so projected coordinates are distinct. **Inference.** Order points by projection. Classify each point by the largest cup and cap ending there. If there is no `r`-cup and no `s`-cap, the cup–cap induction partitions the possible endpoint classes with Pascal recurrence, giving at most `choose(r+s-4,r-2)` points. Therefore one additional point forces an `r`-cup or `s`-cap. Taking `r=s=n`, either monotone-turn chain has all its vertices on the boundary of its convex hull, hence is a convex-independent `n`-set. **Output.** Every general-position set of `choose(2*n-4,n-2)+1` points contains a convex `n`-gon. **Formal anchor.** upper geometric certificate consumed by `erdosSzekeresBounds_upper_projection`. **Downstream use.** N4 places the explicit cardinality in `cardSet n`. **Exceptional cases.** The generic projection handles equal original x-coordinates; no-three-collinear makes every turn strict; cup and cap cases are symmetric. **Trust boundary.** Pascal/Vandermonde arithmetic and the chain-to-convexity implication are explicit proof obligations.
